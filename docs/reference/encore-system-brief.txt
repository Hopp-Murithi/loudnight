 Executive summary

  Encore is an AI programming director that fills a venue's empty nights, using Qloo's taste
  data to decide what to run and then executing it. A bar, café, restaurant or small music
  venue enters its address and its slow night. Encore reads the neighbourhood's taste, finds
  what nobody nearby is offering, invents a recurring event backed by evidence, finds the
  talent, produces the launch kit, and measures who actually came.
  One line: Qloo knows what a neighbourhood loves. Encore turns that into people through
  the door on a Tuesday.
  Why it wins:

      It shows the hackathon's thesis on screen. A "Generic AI vs Encore" toggle runs the
      same venue twice. A plain LLM suggests trivia night; Encore suggests a specific night
      with affinity scores behind every claim.
      Qloo is the engine, not decoration. Location insights, demographics, cross-domain
      affinity, entity search and popularity all drive decisions.
      It closes the loop. Decide, execute, promote, measure, improve. Other tools do one of
      those steps.
      It is a real business. Venues already pay for promotion, booking and POS software.
      Encore sells the missing piece: knowing what to run.
      It avoids crowded categories. Travel planners, restaurant finders, gift and playlist bots
      will flood the gallery. Encore serves the venue owner, not the consumer.

  Target submission: Oct 29. Judging Nov 2­16, winners Nov 23. First prize $15,000.

 The problem

  Most small venues are busy two or three nights a week and run near-empty on the rest,
  while rent, staff and stock cost the same every night. The fix everyone reaches for is an
  event, but owners pick it by guesswork.

      They don't know their neighbourhood's taste. They know their regulars, not the
      thousands of people within walking distance who never come in.
      They copy what's nearby. Four bars on one street all run trivia, so none of them wins
      Tuesday.
      Generic AI makes it worse, not better. Ask any chatbot and it suggests the same safe
      ideas for every postcode: trivia, karaoke, open mic.
      Execution is the second wall. Even with a good idea, the owner still has to find a
      performer, design a poster, write posts and get the word out, usually alone.
      Nobody measures it. Nights are judged on gut feel, so good ideas get dropped too
      early and bad ones run too long.
  The result: owners lose money on dead nights, local performers miss paid gigs, and
  neighbourhoods have less to do. Encore attacks all three.

 The solution: the owner's journey end to end

  The owner spends about two minutes on input and gets a ready-to-run 4-week event
  series back. Worked example: The Copper Room, a bar in Brooklyn. Tuesdays are dead.
   1. Set up (2 minutes). The owner gives the address, the slow night, a break-even

      headcount ("I need 40 people"), what the space can host (stage, screen, sound,
      kitchen) and a talent budget. Google Places fills in type, hours and photos
      automatically.
   2. Watch the agent think (about 60 seconds). A live trace shows each step: reading the
      neighbourhood, checking competitors, generating concepts. Every finding carries its
      source.
   3. Pick a concept. Three concept cards appear, each with a name, a one-line pitch,
      evidence chips ("Afrobeats 3.4× affinity within 2 km"), a fit score, estimated cost and a
      reason it beats what's nearby.
   4. Review the series plan. The chosen concept becomes 4 weekly dates on a calendar,
      each with a variation so regulars keep coming back.
   5. Book talent. Encore shortlists performers whose fan base overlaps this audience, at
      the right popularity tier, and drafts a personal outreach message for each.
   6. Launch. One click produces the event page with RSVP and QR code, a poster, posts for
      Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and Google Business Profile, themed menu specials and
      an ad targeting brief.

7. Run the night. Guests scan the QR code at the door to check in. The owner logs spend
    if they want.

8. Learn. After each night Encore compares turnout with the break-even target and

The owner never faces a blank page. Every step ends in something they can approve, edit

How the agent plans

Encore runs a fixed pipeline of six steps, and Claude chooses which tools to call inside
each step. A fixed spine keeps runs fast and predictable for judges; tool choice inside
each step keeps it genuinely agentic.

                                                                                   Page 4 of 15
Encore -- Full System Brief

1. Scout the venue. Google Places resolves the address to coordinates, venue type,
    hours, rating and photos.

2. Read the neighbourhood. Qloo insights are queried with the venue's location and a
    radius (default 2 km) across artists, films, TV, brands, places, books, podcasts and tags,
    plus the area's demographic skew. The output is a taste fingerprint: the top over-
    indexing entities and tags per domain, with affinity scores.

3. Find the gap. Google Places lists nearby competing venues; Ticketmaster's Discovery
    API lists events nearby on the target weekday. Claude extracts what themes are
    already covered so concepts avoid them.

4. Generate concepts. Claude combines two or three high-affinity signals from different
    domains into a night concept, then re-queries Qloo with those signals to confirm the
    overlap is real. It produces 6 candidates internally and keeps the top 3.

5. Score and explain. Each concept gets a score from the formula below. Each evidence
    chip stores the Qloo request and response it came from.

6. Plan and equip. The chosen concept becomes a 4-week series, talent shortlist, launch
    kit and ad brief.

Concept score (0­100):

  Score = 40 \cdot AudienceFit + 20 \cdot Novelty + 20 \cdot VenueFit + 20
  \cdot CostFit

    AudienceFit = normalised Qloo affinity of the concept's signals in this area.
    Novelty = 1 minus overlap with themes found in the gap scan.
    VenueFit = how well the concept matches the space (stage, screen, kitchen, capacity).
    CostFit = estimated talent and setup cost against the owner's budget.

The grounding rule. Claude may only state a number that exists in a stored Qloo response.
A validator checks every evidence chip against its response before the card is shown. If a
claim has no receipt, it is dropped. This is the technical answer to "how do we know it isn't
making this up?"

Engineering decisions

    Models: Claude Sonnet 5.5 for orchestration and concept reasoning; Claude Haiku 5.5
    for bulk copy (posts, captions, outreach) to keep cost and latency down.
    Long runs: each pipeline step is its own server call, driven from the client and
    streamed, so no single function hits Vercel's time limit.
    Posters without an image model: designed HTML/SVG templates rendered to PNG
    with Satori, filled with the concept's name, palette and copy. Fast, consistent and on-
    brand.
    Speed for judges: demo venues are precomputed and cached; a fresh run on a new
    address takes about a minute.
    Abuse and cost control: per-IP rate limits on fresh runs; Qloo responses reused from
    cache.
    Open source: MIT licence; API keys only in environment variables.

What makes it unique:

    It serves the supply side. Almost every taste-AI product recommends places to
    consumers. Encore helps the place become worth recommending.
    Cross-domain concepts. Qloo's real power is linking music, film, food and brands.
    Encore's concepts are built from those links, which no single-domain tool can produce.
    Receipts on every claim. The grounding rule makes the output auditable, which is rare
    in agent demos.
    Outcome data moat. Check-ins tie taste to real attendance. Over time Encore learns
    which taste signals actually convert, which Qloo alone does not capture.

How to protect the edge before Oct 30: run Encore at one or two real venues, even small
ones, and put the real check-in numbers in the submission. An idea can be repeated; a real
pilot result cannot be copied in time.

    options for every venue.

collectives, and sell through POS and hospitality software integrations. Kenya and other
markets follow once Qloo's location coverage there is confirmed.

Demo script and submission checklist

The demo must make the case in two minutes without any explanation from Hopp.

Two-minute walkthrough

1. 0:00­0:15, the hook. "This bar in Brooklyn is empty every Tuesday. Here's what a
    normal AI tells the owner." Show the generic answer: trivia night.

2. 0:15­0:45, Encore runs. Same venue, live trace: neighbourhood read, competitor gap,
    concepts forming.

3. 0:45­1:10, the evidence. Open a concept card. Tap an evidence chip to show the actual
    Qloo result behind it.

4. 1:10­1:35, execution. Pick the concept: 4-week calendar, talent shortlist, poster and
    posts appear.

5. 1:35­1:50, the door. Show the event page, an RSVP, and a QR check-in updating the
    dashboard.
